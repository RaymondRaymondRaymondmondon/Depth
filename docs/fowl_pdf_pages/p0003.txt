• Birds shot is the score (each bird counts once, credited to whoever fired the killing
shot; specials count more, see Birds).
• Money comes from birds (by species) and from gambling; it buys guns, attachments,
and Slop. A player can lead in money and lose on birds.
• Ties break on accuracy.
• Perfect wave: shooting every bird of a wave before anyone else does pays a bonus and
a crowd cheer.
The range
The whole game is one building and the marsh in front of it: the Fowl Play Gun Club, a
timber clubhouse with a long covered porch facing a marsh at dusk.
The porch: six stalls
• Six stalls side by side along the porch rail, 3 m wide each, divided by waist-high wooden
partitions so everyone can see everyone. Each stall has a numbered post, a hook for
spare guns, an ammo tray, and a little scoreboard flap.
• The marsh in front is 200 m wide and 150 m deep: reeds at the near edge (where birds
flush), open water, a few dead trees, a hill at the back where the sun sets, and a
treeline. Birds fly across the whole field, so a bird on the far left is reachable from stall
