Overview
Fowl Play is a 1-6 player first-person 3D bird-shooting competition on the Deep Arcade's
Slop shelf, built on the bones of the classic NES light-gun duck shooter. Players stand in a
row of stalls on a covered shooting porch overlooking a marsh, birds are released over the
field, and everyone shoots at the same sky. Between hunts there are 45 seconds to walk
the room behind the stalls: buy guns and attachments, spin the mystery-gun machine,
gamble winnings at slots and scratchers, and visit the Slop Shop for cosmetics and dirty
tricks to use on everyone else.
What it takes from the light-gun classic: the marsh, the reeds, the flushed birds flying up
and away, the round-by-round escalation, the hit counter at the bottom of the screen, the
dog who retrieves your birds (and laughs at your misses), and the plastic toy gun. What it
adds: a real 3D space shared by six players, money, an arsenal of 30 guns built in that
same toy style, gambling, and sabotage.
The look of the guns. Every gun in the game is drawn as a chunky toy light-gun version of
itself: two-tone grey moulded plastic (a lighter grey body over a darker grey barrel and
grip), a ribbed grip, a stubby red trigger, a ribbed slide on top, visible screw bosses, and a
red maker's stamp on the side (an invented brand, the "Zappa" logo, never a real one). A
shotgun is a toy shotgun in grey and red; a gatling gun is a toy gatling gun; the ray gun is a
toy ray gun. Real gun types keep their real silhouettes so players can recognize them; the
plastic is the joke and the identity.
Design pillars
1. One sky, six guns. Everyone shoots the same birds, so every shot is a race and every
kill is stolen from somebody.
2. Money changes everything. Birds pay; the intermission spends; the arsenal and the
gambling decide who's ahead by round 10.
3. Slop with craft. Crab cannons and pigeon decoys, but the shooting feels tight and the
economy is balanced enough to compete on.
