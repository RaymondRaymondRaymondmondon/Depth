Overview
Red Tide is a four-player 3D survival shooter in the Deep Arcade, built on the bones of
Black Ops 2 and 3 Zombies but with no zombies: the players are divers dropped into living
ecosystems, earning money by killing beasts while the food web, the blood in the water,
and the enemies drawn by their gunfire close in around them. Every map is an ecosystem
of about 40 beasts and 12 flora that eat, hide, breed, and hunt each other whether or not
the players are watching.

1. The food web is the enemy. Zombies come because it's a round; beasts come because
you made blood, noise, or a corpse. The ecosystem runs without you and reacts to
everything you do.
2. Every kill is a decision. Small kills are safe scrip; big kills bring bigger things; too many
kills bring the things that eat everything.
3. Enemies are prey too. The human and unhuman factions that hunt you are also on the
menu, and the smartest play is often to let the reef eat them.
4. Each map is an ecosystem first and a level second. Layouts are built around where
creatures live, feed, and hide.
5. Zombies pacing. Fast, readable, arcade-loud: 60-second tides early, minutes-long late,
always a way to run, always a reason to stop and buy.
The five maps (smallest to largest): the Sunken Ship, the Underwater Cave, the Coral
Reef, Atlantis the Forgotten City, and Approaching the Void.
Session: 4 players (or 1-3 with scaled quotas), 20-60 minutes, ending when every player is
dead. The score is the tide reached and the scrip earned, and both pay arcade tokens.
Core loop and rules
A match runs in tides. Each tide has a kill quota; meeting it ends the tide, pays a bonus,
and starts the next one harder. Between tides the water calms for 20 seconds, the only
reliable time to buy, build, and revive.

Health, downs, revives
• 100 HP, regenerating 4s after the last hit at 20 HP/s (Zombies-style). Juggernaut Tonic
raises HP to 250.
• At O HP a diver is downed: they float with the Cormorant only, for 45 s, bleeding into
the water (a downed diver is a strong blood source, which is the danger and the
drama).
• Any teammate can revive in 4 s (2 s with Quick Brine). A revived diver loses all tonics
unless they hold a Salt Charm that keeps them.
• If every diver is down, the match ends. Solo has one self-revive per Quick Brine bought
(three per match).
• Air. There is no air timer; divers breathe indefinitely. The pressure against camping in a
corner comes from blood, scent, and the enemy alarm instead.
Doors, power, and the map opening up
• Doors and debris cost 750-2,500 scrip and open permanently for everyone. Each map
opens in stages from the start pocket outward.
• Power (the generator, the tide mill, the temple's crystal) turns on the Forge, the
Locker's second location, tonic machines beyond the first two, and the map's traps.
Turning on power also wakes the ecosystem's larger predators, so the choice of when

a shared set of rules: what they eat, what eats them, what they smell, what they hear,
where they live, and who they help. Players and enemy factions are members of the same

The seven layers of the web
1. Trophic layer. The diet graph. Each beast has a hunger meter; hunger raises the weight
of hunting over everything else. Grazers eat flora; predators eat by preference and
size; scavengers eat corpses. A well-fed predator ignores prey, so an area that has just
been hunted through is briefly safer.
2. Blood and scent layer. The map holds a 3D scent grid (2 m cells). Blood diffuses to
neighboring cells each second and decays 2% per second (1% at tide 10+, 0.5% at tide
20+, so late tides never clear). Every species samples the grid within its scent range
each second; when the total exceeds its threshold, it follows the gradient to the
source. Because thresholds are per species, one dead minnow brings scavengers, a
dead tuna brings sharks, and four dead tuna at once brings the thing that eats sharks.
3. Sound layer. Every weapon has a noise value; explosions and traps are louder. Sound is
a second field with fast decay (20% per second). Species react by temperament:
fearful ones flee it, CUriOUS ones approach it, aggressive ones attack it. Sound also
feeds the enemy alarm (below).
4. Territory layer. Beasts with a home defend a radius around it against anything larger
than prey, including players and enemies, and return home when calm. Nests, burrows,
anemone beds, and wrecks are where fights start without any blood at all.
5. Symbiosis layer. Cleaners reduce the aggression of hosts they service; hosts shelter
symbionts; parasites attach to beasts and divers (slowing, bleeding, or blinding them
until scraped off at a cleaning station). Mimics look like harmless species until touched.
Lures and lights draw curious species.
6. Flora layer. Flora grows, gets grazed, shelters, feeds, and sometimes hunts. Flora
density sets grazer carrying capacity; grazer numbers set predator patrol frequency;
so shooting up a kelp bed or a coral head changes what lives there for the rest of the
match.
7. Enemy layer. Enemy factions are species in the same web: they bleed, they are prey to
apex beasts, they hunt beasts that threaten them, and some of them feed the web on
purpose (chumming, netting).
The blood rules the player learns
• A shot beast bleeds; a dead one bleeds much more. Blood attracts by total amount in
the area, not by the number of kills, so ten small fish and one big fish can call the same
predator.
• Corpses keep bleeding until scavengers finish them (30-120 s by size). Scavengers,
therefore, are the players' friends, and killing them for scrip is the classic mistake.

• Wounded players bleed; downed players bleed heavily. Reviving fast is also ecology.
• Moving out of bloody water is the only real reset; blood follows currents, so the map's
flow tells you where it goes.
• The HUD shows blood as a red tint at the edge of the mask and a small scent meter, and
predators' approach as a pulse from their direction.
The enemy alarm
• Each map region has an alarm value. Gunfire adds the weapon's noise; explosions add
triple; melee adds nothing. It decays 5% per second.
When alarm exceeds x (1 / (1 + 0.1 x tide)) , a roll every 5 s spawns an
enemy patrol at the region's faction entry with chance + 3% x tide (capped at
70%). Higher tides make quieter play matter more.
• Four divers firing in one region fill one alarm four times faster than four divers spread
across four regions. Spreading out lowers enemy pressure but raises blood-in-one-
place risk when someone goes down alone: the coordination trade-off the Sunken Ship
teaches first.
• Enemies that arrive follow the same sound and blood senses as beasts, so they find
you the way the beasts do.
Behavior state machine
Every beast runs: Graze or Rest Investigate Hunt Feed Flee Defend Return
Dead, with transitions driven by hunger, blood, sound, threat size, and home distance.
Schools use boids with a shared leader; packs assign flank and drive roles; swarms are a
single agent with many bodies. Fleeing beasts drop the blood they were about to make: a
wounded fish that escapes leaves a thin trail, which is itself a lure for the next predator.
Population
• Each region has spawn pools per species with a carrying capacity tied to flora density
and tide number.
• Beasts that leave a region to follow blood are replaced slowly at home; beasts killed in a
region are replaced at the region's respawn rate (small fast, apex slow, boss never).
• Predators that overfeed leave; grazers then rebound; predators return. The cycle is
tuned to about 3 minutes so a match feels alive without stalling quotas.
• The boss beast of each map is a single persistent agent with a very high blood
threshold and a home; it wanders more each tide.

Simulation budget
About 300 active agents at full rate (10 Hz decisions, 60 Hz movement) near the players,
and population counters only for regions beyond 120 m. The host runs the simulation;
clients receive agent positions and states with fog filtering by distance. --eco-sim <map>
runs the ecosystem headless and prints population curves, blood levels, and
predator arrival times for a scripted player pattern.
Weapons
Red Tide's arsenal follows Zombies' shape (a fixed starting pistol, wall racks, a random
box, a Pack-a-Punch, and a wonder weapon per map) with underwater guns: gas-driven
dart and needle guns, spearguns, harpoon rifles, and sealed-cartridge powder guns. Every
weapon has a noise value that feeds the alarm and the sound layer, so the loud guns are
the strong ones and the quiet ones are the safe ones.
Stats and rules
• Two weapon slots (three with Mule Brine). Weapons are picked up from racks, the
Locker, or dead enemies.
• Damage is per dart or bolt; rate is rounds per minute; noise 1-10 feeds the alarm; reload
is seconds.
• Ammo is bought at the rack (half the weapon's price) or refilled by a Resupply drop.
Locker weapons can only be refilled by Resupply or the Forge's re-arm.
• Headshots and weak points do (3x with Deadeye Brine).
• Water drag: dart weapons lose damage past 25 m, harpoons past 60 m, powder guns
past 40 m.

Animations
• Reloads are underwater and slow-fast-slow: gas pistols pop the cylinder and thumb in
darts one at a time until full or interrupted; needlers swap a glass cartridge with a hiss
of bubbles; powder guns eject a sealed brass clip that sinks; spearguns cock with both
arms and a visible strain; harpoon rifles winch a bolt from a side quiver; the scatter gun
is loaded shell by shell and can be interrupted. Every reload has an empty and a partial
version and a Speed Brine version 30% faster.
• Firing: bubble bursts from gas guns, a brass flash from powder guns, a line snapping
taut from spearguns.
• Inspect animations show the sea-glass Forge finish.
• Weapon skins: arcade tokens buy finishes (barnacle, verdigris, bone inlay, pearl, red
tide) that apply to all weapons.
The Harpoon Cannon (Harpoon Hour)
A shoulder-mounted salvage cannon with infinite bolts for 30 s, 500 damage, noise 10: the
map's loudest sound, which is the joke and the risk.

Player stats and progression
• In-match stats: kills, headshots, scrip earned, downs, revives, beasts eaten by other
beasts because of you, enemies eaten, highest tide.
• Between matches: an arcade profile rank from 1 to 50 across all Red Tide maps; ranks
unlock charms, skins, and dossier pages. No stat ever changes in-match power; every
match starts from the same spawn-in.
• Dossiers: every beast, flora, and enemy killed or observed for 30 s adds a dossier page
(behavior, diet, weak point, what it fears). The full dossier is the map's real strategy
guide.
Player skins
Four divers with fixed silhouettes, each a Depth character type: the Diver (brass helmet),
the Whaler (slicker and hood), the Stowaway (a patched suit and a bottle on a cord), and
the Mechanic (a riveted hard suit). Skins bought with tokens change suit colors and
helmets (verdigris, red tide, bone, pearl, Atlantean) but never the silhouette, so
teammates and enemies always read who is who.
Enemy factions
Each map has one faction drawn by the alarm and by Hunts. They are intelligent, they
shoot back, and they are also animals in the food web: they bleed, they are hunted, and
some of them use the web against the players. Enemies drop their weapons and 200-600
scrip.

Shared enemy rules
Enemies see, hear, and smell like beasts: they can be lured by chum, blinded by ink, and
distracted by beasts attacking them. Their squad Al prefers a fight against players over
beasts but will turn to defend itself.
Enemies scale with tide: +8% HP per tide, better weapons every 5 tides (Forged
weapons at tide 15+).
An enemy killed by a beast counts for no scrip, but the beast that ate it is now fed and
calmer.

Tiers
• Tier 1 grazers, filter feeders, and detritivores: eat flora and corpses; the base of the
web and the safest scrip.
• Tier 2 small predators: eat tier 1; territorial or venomous species here are the early
hazards.
• Tier 3 mid predators: eat tiers 1-2 and each other; the tide 4-9 pressure.
• Tier 4 apex: eat everything below, including enemies and divers; arrive by blood from
tide 4, wander from tide 10.
• Tier 5 boss: one per map, size 6, a home and a habit; a Predator Hunt can call it.

What a beast contributes to the web
Killing a beast removes it from its region's population, adds its corpse (a blood source
scavengers reduce), and changes who patrols there. Killing all the cleaners in a zone raises
every host's aggression; killing all the grazers empties the zone of the predators' reason to
come; killing an apex empties a zone of fear for one tide, and then something else moves
in.

smallest map and the tutorial for the whole game: tight corridors, flora that bites, fish
whose blood calls sharks, and an alarm that fills fast when four divers fire in one room.

The web in motion
• The sprat chain: algae mat krill sprats trevally and barracuda bull shark tiger
shark. Shooting sprats for cheap scrip is the map's first trap.
• The cleaner rule: the galley and the outside cleaning stations calm the hosts that visit
(groupers, barracuda, turtles). Wiping them out makes the mid tiers hostile
everywhere.
• The crocodile problem: the only air inside is its lair. Feed it a turtle (shoot one near the
pocket) and it eats for 60 s.
• Wreckers feed the reef: a Wrecker squad dying in the salon is 80 blood in a closed
room; the bull shark arrives 30 s later. Let the reef kill them outside instead.
• The Goliath: everything that flees runs to the engine room's shadows and gets eaten
there. The Forge is safe only right after it has fed.
Definition of Done
• All 40 beasts and 12 flora exist as data and models; --eco-sim ship 20 shows the
sprat chain calling a bull shark within 90 s of 15 sprat kills in the salon.
• A four-player LAN match reaches tide 10 with the crocodile, a Wrecker Hunt, and the
Goliath's inhale all seen.

Level mechanics
• Rockfall. Gunfire in the Chimney and the Cathedral has a 5% chance per loud shot
(noise 6+) to drop a stalactite somewhere nearby: 200 damage to whatever is under it,
beast, enemy, or diver. The Resonator drops them on purpose.
• Air chambers. The Dry Chambers hold the map's insects and amphibians; divers walk
there, slowly, and the toad's tongue reaches from the pool.
• Echo. The cave counts as one region for the alarm; the Drowned come from the Dry
Chambers.
• Bloodvine. A red alga that smells like blood to the web: shooting a patch makes
predators investigate it. The cave's built-in decoy, and its built-in trap when it grows
near the Forge.

Level mechanics
• Coral corridors limit fire lanes to 8-12 m; loud guns are wasted, spearguns and knives
shine.
• Eel holes line the corridors: passing within 1 m risks a moray or ribbon eel strike;
Vulture's Eye shows occupied holes.
• Reacher coral grabs a diver who brushes it and holds until a teammate cuts them loose
(or the coral is shot to O); it also grabs beasts and enemies, which feed it.
• The sand flats have no cover and full sightlines: the loud-gun zone, and the place
where a downed diver's blood spreads fastest.
• Dolphins. The pod follows the divers and drives sharks off while nobody has shot a
dolphin. One shot turns the pod hostile for the match.
• Crown-of-thorns eat coral: leaving them alive erodes corridors (opening lanes for
sharks); killing them is 40 blood each.

Presentation and technology
Red Tide is first-person 3D in raylib, extending the code that already draws the parkour
side's 3D Abyss ( abyss . cpp ), with every mesh, texture, and animation generated in code,
per Depth's no-assets rule. The look is inked low-poly: flat-shaded meshes with a screen-
space ink outline and hatching, so a screenshot still reads as Depth.
Art
Style. Low-poly meshes with flat shading, a post-process ink outline (depth and normal
edge detection, line weight by distance), Bayer stipple in shadow, and the same paper-
tone palette rules as the salon. Underwater fog by depth, volumetric light shafts,
caustics projected on surfaces near the surface, and particle marine snow everywhere.
• Creatures are procedural meshes. A CreatureBui1der makes a body from a lathed
profile curve, adds fins, limbs, shells, tentacles, spines, and eyes from parameter sets,
and generates a 3-band material (base, belly, accent) from the species record. Forty
species per map is forty parameter sets plus a dozen body plans (fusiform fish, flat
fish, eel, ray, shark, turtle, crocodilian, crab, shrimp, cephalopod, jelly, worm, amphibian,
seal, whale).
• Animation. Fish swim by a traveling sine along the spine; rays flap; eels undulate; crabs
walk on inverse-kinematics legs; cephalopods run Chains on every arm; reptiles and
mammals use a four-limb gait. Attack, flee, feed, and death clips per body plan; blood
as ribbon particles that follow the scent grid's flow so what the player sees is what the
web smells.
• Weapons and hands. First-person hands and guns are procedural meshes with the
reload clips from the Weapons section; the sea-glass Forge finish is a material swap.

Sound design
Red Tide's sound is the ecosystem made audible: every species has a voice, every kill has
a wet consequence, and the music is the round timer. All of it is synthesized through the
cue registry and bUSes defined in the Depth master reference.
Underwater mix rules
• Everything is low-passed and reverberant; gunfire is a thump and a hiss of bubbles, not
a crack, except the powder guns, which crack and then boom in the water.
• Distance is muffling: sounds beyond 40 m lose their highs entirely, so the direction of a
far predator is felt more than heard.
• The diver's own breathing is always present and quickens with low HP and nearby
blood.

Effects
• Every beast has a voice cue (clicks, grunts, croaks, whistles, or silence by design), a
pain cue, a death cue, and a feeding cue (tearing, crunching, slurping). Feeding cues are
loud by design: they tell the players where the corpses are being eaten and how many
things are eating.
• Blood has a sound: a wet bloom on a kill, a spreading hiss as a plume grows.
• Weapons: fire, reload steps, empty click, the Forge's crush and hiss, the Locker's eel
snap and the chest sinking, the Harpoon Cannon's boom.
• Tonics: each machine has a jingle (the Zombies tradition), a bottle clink, and a gulp.
• Drops: a bell for a drop appearing, a distinct sting per drop.
• The alarm: no sound tells the player the alarm value, but the faction's arrival has a tell:
the Wreckers' cutters scraping the hull, the Drowned's bell, the Raiders/ drum, the Lost
Ones' marching, the Remnant's airlock cycling.
• Tells for hazards: the sand worm's rumble, the Reacher coral's creak, the bobbit
worm's hiss, the toad's croak, the Wyrmls grate rattle, the leviathan's lure hum.
HUD and UI: the tide bell, the quota tick, the scrip counter's coin, the down and revive
stings.
Accessibility
Every audio tell has a visual: a directional pulse on the mask, a caption for barks, and a
scent meter for the blood strings.

The Goliath (the Sunken Ship)
• Arena. The Engine Room until tide 12; after that it wanders and the ship is the arena.
15,000 HP; front plates halve damage; the gills behind the plates are the weak point,
exposed for 2 s after an Inhale and after a Tail Slam.
• Phase 1 (100-60%). Inhale (a 1.5 s windup as the gills close and water rushes; pulls one
diver from 4 m into its mouth; the team has 5 s to deal 150 damage to the gills or the
diver is swallowed and downed), Lunge (140, bleed), Boom (a 10-noise pulse that stuns
1 s in 10 m). It stays within 12 m of its home.
• Phase 2 (60-30%). Adds Tail Slam (90, knocks back 6 m, drops loose cargo on three
marked squares), Boom every 3 s, and calls two Groupers. It roams the whole room; the
Forge can't be used while it is within 8 m.
• Phase 3 (30-0%). Enraged: speed Inhale every 8 s, a swallow is an instant down. It
breaks the aft bulkhead and chases into the Stern.
• Arena rules. Shooting the luminous algae mat off the wall (300 damage) blinds it for 6 s
because it hunts by the mat's light; cycling the power switch stuns it 2 s (once per
fight); loose cargo can be shot to drop on it for 200.
• Team plan. One diver baits the Inhale from 4 m; two shoot the gills in the 2 s window;
one handles the Groupers and the cargo. Reward: 1,500 base scrip x the tide multiplier,

Controls (keyboard and mouse; gamepad mapped the same)
Move, swim up and down (space and Ctrl), sprint (shift, with a stamina bar that only shows
while sprinting), aim, fire, reload, melee (V), tactical (G), swap weapon (1, 2, 3), interact (E,

teammates see, with a voice line), charm (T), scoreboard (Tab).
The divers and their quips
The four divers are four suits, not four characters: the Diver, the Whaler, the Stowaway,
and the Mechanic, each with a fixed silhouette and a voice, and each replaceable by a
skin. Their lines are about the situation, not their lives, so any skin can say them; the fun is
that they talk to each other.
How quips work
• Each diver has a voice (a formant synth pitch and rhythm) and a line set per situation.
Lines are picked from the set, never repeated within 3 minutes, and only one diver
speaks at a time.
• Callbacks: about a third of lines are two-part: one diver speaks, and a teammate within
15 m answers 1-2 s later with a reply from their own set. The four voices differ in tone:
the Diver is dry, the Whaler is gruff, the Stowaway is cheerful and slightly drunk, the
Mechanic is nervous and precise.
Quips are subtitled and never interrupt a tell or a Hunt announcement.

Enemy spawning and Hunts
Alarm squads
1. Each region tracks its alarm (constants in the engine workbook). Above threshold, a
roll every 5 s spawns a squad with the tide's chance; the alarm halves when it
succeeds.
2. The squad spawns at the region's nearest faction entry (each map lists them) that is at
least 35 m from every diver and out of every diver's sight; if none qualifies, at the map's
default entry.
3. Squad size is 3 at tides 4-9, 4 at 10-19, 5 at 20+, drawn from the faction's composition
(never two of the specialist unit in one squad).
4. Squads path toward the region where the alarm fired, then toward the nearest diver's
last known position; they lose the trail after 20 s without sight, scent, or sound, and
then patrol the faction's route.
5. At most 2 alarm squads are alive at once (3 at tide 20+); further rolls are skipped.
6. A squad wiped by beasts counts as resolved: the alarm is not raised by their deaths.
Hunts
• Tide 5, 10, 15, and every 5th tide after: a Faction Hunt unless a Predator Hunt is rolled
(50% from tide 10, from tide 20).
• Faction Hunt. The quota is replaced by "eliminate the Hunt force": 10 units at tide 5, 12
at 10, 14 at 15, 16 at 20+, plus the faction's Hunt leader. The force spawns in three waves
30 s apart from the map's Hunt staging point; the leader spawns with the third wave.

The Hunt ends when the force and the leader are dead or eaten. Reward: a Resupply

Divers swim at 2.0 m/s, sprint at 3.4 for 6 s on a stamina bar that only appears while
sprinting and refills in 8 s, rise and dive at 1.4, and reach full speed in 0.4 s. Aiming slows
swimming to 60%; harpoons and spearguns to 40%. In air chambers they walk at 1.6 with
no jump and a 1 m step-up. A hold can be broken early by mashing (six taps) or by a
teammate dealing 15% of the holder's HP. Knockbacks unsettle aim for 0.6 s. Purchases
over 1,000 need a 1 s hold; reviving requires holding still; downed divers drift-crawl at 0.6.
Past the abyss edge the pull lasts 5 s with a warning at the first second.
Progression, in one paragraph
A match pays 10 tokens, a win (tide 10+) 25, and 2 per tide past 10; firsts pay more (tide 20
on a map 50, a boss 30, a quest 100, a full dossier 75). Ranks cost 50 x rankA1.6 tokens
each, so a good match earns 40-70 and rank 50 is about 150 matches. Ranks unlock
charms, skins, pouch slots, and three bonus dossier pages; two charms (Lucky Locker, Fair
Shares) come from tide milestones instead. Cosmetics cost 100-300. Nothing bought or
earned changes in-match power.
The how-to-play page (final text)
One scrolling page in the arcade menu, styled as a page torn from the ship's log, chalk
headings, and one diagram. This is the game's only teaching; nothing in a match explains
itself.
THE OWNERS ' STANDING ORDERS FOR DIVERS
1. The job. You and up to three others go down with a pistol, a knife, two charges, and five
hundred scrip. You kill what lives there and the Owners pay by the head. Every tide sets a
quota; meet it and the next tide is worth more and wants more of you. There is no end.
There is only how deep you get.
Page 89 of 90=== PAGE 0090.txt
Red Tide — Arcade Game 5 Design Document
2. Scrip. Ten for a hit, the bounty for a kill, more for the eyes and the gills. Scrip opens
doors, buys guns off the walls, pulls from DavVs Locker, pours tonics, and feeds the
Pressure Forge. It's yours alone; the Fair Shares charm is the only way to split it. Spend
between tides. Twenty seconds of calm is all you get.
3. Blood. Everything you shoot bleeds, and everything you kill bleeds more. Blood drifts
with the current and the water remembers it. Small things smell a little blood and come to
eat. Big things need a lot of blood, but ten small kills make as much as one big one.
Scavengers clean it up; kill them and nothing does. Watch the red at the edge of your
mask, watch the vial, and when the pulse starts, leave.
4. Noise. Guns are heard. Fire enough in one place and someone comes to see who's
spending the reef: Wreckers, the Drowned, the tribe, the Lost Ones, what's left of the
station. They hunt you, and the reef hunts them, and the smart diver stands where the
second thing happens. Four divers firing in one room bring them four times faster than
four rooms. Knives bring nobody.
5. Going down. At zero you're floating with the pistol and forty-five seconds, and you're
bleeding into the water the whole time. A friend can pull you up in four; fewer with Quick
Brine. Dead is when everyone's down. Cold Coffin and Quick Brine buy second chances;
nothing buys a third.
6. The rest you learn by dying. Which coral grabs. Which light isn't a light. Which fish is on
your side until you shoot it. Which door the shark can't fit through. The dossier fills in as
you go: every beast you kill or watch for half a minute tells you what it eats, what eats it,
where it's soft, and what it's afraid of. Read it between matches. It's the only map that
matters.
Diagram caption (a chalk drawing of a diver, a dead fish, and three arrows): One kill: a little
blood, the scavengers come. Ten kills in one place: the shark comes. A diver down:
everything comes. Move, spread out, keep it quiet, and let the water forget you.
Below the page, in smaller chalk: Controls are on the back. Good luck. The Owners are not
responsible for anything that happens below the surface, including the surface.
