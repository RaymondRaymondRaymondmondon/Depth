The Deep
The Deep
Oct 6, 2026 • @Phillip Argall
Overview
The Deep (working title in the sources: Project Nautilus / Nautilus: Depths of Wake) is a 4-
player online co-op action game about surviving the deep ocean. It combines the deep-
sea progression of Subnautica, the claustrophobic crew teamwork of a 20,000 Leagues
Under the Sea setting, and the systemic, horror-tinged food webs of The Trawl (the
reference design The Trawl — Arcade Game 2).
The crew starts adrift on an inflatable life raft and rows to the derelict submarine
Nautilus, which becomes their mobile base. Instead of building stationary habitats, they
repair the sub room by room, reinforce its hull to survive greater pressure, and venture
out into a fully simulated ocean to gather what they need. They're armed with little more
than survival knives and spearguns, so survival depends on evasion, deterrence, and
teamwork rather than firepower.
• Setting: an oceanic alien world of abyssal trenches, volcanic vents, and bioluminescent
caverns, plus one persistent surface island.
• Scope: 4-player co-op (1 to 4 players). Genre: action, built on survival-crafting systems.
• Engine: Unreal Engine 5 (confirmed), with a custom simulation layer for the ecosystem.
Core pillars
• The ship is alive. The Nautilus is the crew's only safe haven, oxygen refinery, and
mobile base. It needs people to helm, repair, pump, balance power, track sonar, manage
fuel, and upgrade. Every room has a mechanical purpose.
• The food web reacts. The sea is simulated, not scripted. Noise, light, and blood draw
predators. The players are part of the food chain, and not at the top.
• Sound is a system (the Wake). Water carries sound efficiently. Every engine throttle,
thruster burst, tool use, and dropped item writes noise into the water that predators
can hear.
• Deep trophic ecology. Biomes run on persistent food webs with energy conservation,
not spawn tables. Over-hunting collapses prey populations and pushes predators onto
the players.

• State-driven creature Al. Creatures act on internal drives (hunger, fear, territorial urge,
acoustic irritation, daily migration) instead of scripted patrol paths.
• Creatures live their own lives. Species interact without the player: pack hunting,
cleaning stations, territorial fights, decoy parasitism, and whale-fall decay.
• Progression through pressure. Depth is the main gate. Going deeper requires
pressure-resistant hulls, thermal insulation, and specialized vehicles built from what
the crew harvests in hostile biomes.
The start and progression
The opening. Players spawn at night on a vulnerable inflatable life raft in the middle of a
dark, rolling ocean. They row through dangerous surface waters, navigating by the faint,
dying emergency lights of a massive derelict submarine breaching the surface: the
Nautilus. The first goal is to board it, restore primary power, and dive before a surface
storm or a surface-hunting leviathan destroys them.
The loop: gather, craft, upgrade, descend. Crew members free-dive or take small vehicles
out to gather titanium, copper, metals, organic matter, bioluminescent flora, deep-sea
minerals, and blueprints. They bring it back to repair the sub's rooms (engine room,
hydroponics bay, moonpool, and the rest) and to reinforce the hull. A stronger hull lets the
Nautilus descend into new pressure depths, where it becomes the staging ground for
even deeper biomes. Support vehicles (agile mini-subs for tight caverns, heavy exosuits
for deep hauling) reach places the Nautilus can't.
Depth phases. Progression is gated by pressure, with one phase per underwater biome.
Each phase ends with a hull upgrade that raises the Nautilus's crush depth to the bottom
of the next biome. Every upgrade combines a new material from the current biome with
materials from shallower ones, so the crew keeps returning to earlier biomes (full recipes
in Crafting, resources, and upgrades).

Sound is a core gameplay system, not just atmosphere. Everything the crew does leaves
an Acoustic Footprint (measured in dB) and builds Wake Heat (W), a hidden heat value
written into the local water column that attracts predators, up to the biome's leviathan.

Wake tiers. Higher Wake draws bigger threats, in order:
1. Minor disturbances (30 to 50 dB): attract curiOUS grazing fish and opportunistic
scavengers.
2. Moderate mechanical noise (50 to 80 dB): draws aggressive mesopredators (sharks,
hunting eels, pack hunters).
3. High-energy impacts or blood spills (80+ dB, or chemical spills): trigger a biome-wide
territorial response from the apex leviathan.
Player noise. Mining a node with a laser makes a high-pitched whine. A speargun shot
makes a distinct thwack. Even swimming with flippers makes low-frequency water
displacement that nearby stalker-class predators can track. Firing weapons and
wounding fish both build Wake, which is why fighting anything bigger than the player is
strongly discouraged.
Living ecosystem simulation
The ocean runs on a background simulation (called the Dynamic Trophic Simulation, or
DTS, engine in v4) built from five interlocking systems: biomass conservation, sound
propagation, scent drifting on currents, state-driven creature Al, and daily vertical
migration. The sources describe it as a living food web inspired by the reactive
environments of The Trawl, not a set of scripted spawns.
Biomass and trophic cascades
Each biome has a total biomass pool split across five levels:
(producers) + Bl (primary consumers) + B2 (mesopredators) + B3
B total
(apex leviathans) + B4 (decomposers)
• Producers (BO) regrow from the biome's energy source (E_env): sunlight in shallow
water, heat at volcanic vents, chemical energy in the trenches. Producer biomass sets
each region's creature population caps.
• Energy passes up each level at about 12% efficiency (n = 0.12). Grazers eat biomass to
reproduce; predators need calories to keep up stamina and aggression.
• Over-harvesting cascade: if players over-hunt grazers (Bl) for rations, for example
schools of Chromis Gliders, Bl falls below the biome's stability threshold (Bl,min).
• Starving mesopredators: without prey, B2 predators enter the STARVING state. Their
perception radius grows by 350%, their fear threshold drops to zero, and they attack
other species, players, and the Nautilus hull, trying to break into the Larder and

strikes any moving thing.

displays (flashing bioluminescence, growls) come first, then lethal attacks.

Day, night, and the moon
• Daytime: sunlight reaches the upper layers, flora blooms and photosynthesizes, and
apex predators stay deeper. Sunlit zones are relatively safe.
• Dusk (19:00): surface photosynthesis stops and phytoplankton bioluminescent blooms
begin. Deep-water species and abyssal leviathans rise 600 to 1,200 m to feed in the
dark, and bioluminescence replaces sunlight.
• Dawn (05:00): sunlight pushes predators back down into the deep trenches, making
the upper biomes safe again.
• Full moon: pulls huge plankton blooms to the surface, bringing bait fish and their
predators with them (like moonlight driving deep-sea movement in The Trawl).
Night dives yield rare bioluminescent flora, at extreme risk.
Creatures interacting with each other
Species interact whether or not the crew is around: cooperative pack hunting, cleaning
stations, territorial fights, decoy parasitism, and whale-fall decay sequences. One worked
example is the Barbed Reef-Snapper flushing prey toward ambushing Moray Creepers
(see Featured species).
Gathering, hunting, and weapons
The crew is not a military force. Fighting anything larger than a player is strongly
discouraged, because every shot and WOUnd builds Wake and draws something bigger.

Deployable gadgets
• Flares: distract light-drawn predators.
• Flashbangs (flash flares): blind light-sensitive deep-sea predators that rely on
enormous eyes; they overload the Flash-Stalker Leviathan's vision and force it to
retreat.
• Acoustic Decoy Buoys: broadcast a 90 dB fake signature (a humming engine or a
wounded fish) to lure blind predators away. A buoy is destroyed after 45 seconds, and
predators go back to tracking ambient noise.
• Chum Buckets: lead predators away from resource nodes.
• Mineral detector: handheld; finds mid-tier resources.
• Stasis rifle: see Gathering, hunting, and weapons.
The ten ecosystems
Nine underwater biomes stacked by depth with no gaps or overlaps, plus the surface
Island. Each biome has exactly 20 unique flora and 60 unique fauna, and its food web is
closed: every animal's prey and predators appear in that biome's own tables (alongside
base food sources like plankton, detritus, or marine snow).

2. The Kelp Labyrinth (50 to 150 m)
•
Layout: towering iron-hard kelp stalks reach from the muddy floor to the surface,
forming narrow mazes. Ordinary kelp fouling a propeller can be cut with the Survival
Knife; Iron-Kelp tangles need the Heated Blade. Clearings hold copper, quartz, and
titanium nodes. The tangled dreadnought hangs here.
Look: shadowy emerald water, filtered sunbeams through the canopy, floating spores,
dark shapes between giant rubbery trunks.
Hazards: propeller tangles, very low visibility, ambush predators disguised as kelp
fronds (Wraiths, the Tangle-Serpent Leviathan).

• Food web: Iron-Kelp, the other macro-algae, and phytoplankton feed the plankton, the
sluggish grazers (Kelp-Cow, Kelp-Grazer Manatee, Holdfast Hog), and armored snails.

4. The Thermal Vents (300 to 500 m)
•
Layout: volcanic ridges, black smoker chimneys, and basalt plateaus spewing
superheated mineral plumes. Boiling water pillars damage hulls with heat and cause
turbulence. Rich in copper and thermal energy.
Look: hazy, heat-distorted water, red magma glowing through cracks in the basalt,
black mineral clouds, armor-plated fauna.
Hazards: extreme heat stress on the hull (needs Thermal Hull Shielding), sudden
geysers, turbulence that throws subs against volcanic glass, and the Boiler Worm
erupting from the rock.

5. The Drowned Forest (500 to 700 m)
• Layout: a sunken prehistoric forest of massive petrified trunks, thick mud beds, and
rotting canopy debris hanging in the water. Narrow passages between stone trees; the
Wood-Hag rearranges trunks into labyrinths.
• Look: murky brown water, floating silt clouds, twisted petrified roots, hanging fungus,
flickering ember-like glows.
• Hazards: propellers fouled by floating rot fibers (needs anti-fouling), battery-draining
Ampere Leeches and Silt-Cap mycelium, ambush predators that mimic tree trunks.
• Food web: wood microbes and fungi (Peat Mold, Trunk Bracket, Rot-Wood Parasite)
feed Ember Motes and timber grazers such as the Mud-Crawler, Heartwood Gnawer,
and Timber Shipworm. Trunk-mimicking ambushers like the Snag-Trunk Eel, Driftwood

• Layout: fields of razor-sharp quartz and silicate crystal pillars growing in geometric

7. The Brine Pools (900 to 1,200 m)
• Layout: craters filled with ultra-dense, toxic, super-salty brine that forms still "lakes"
on the sea floor. Subs float on the brine's surface; sinking into it corrodes the hull
(needs Anti-Corrosion Coating).
• Look: milky green-yellow toxic "rivers" under dark water, skeletons floating on the
brine surface, mirror-like boundaries between the two fluids.
• Hazards: hull corrosion, zero visibility inside the brine, amphibious predators
ambushing from below the brine layer, acid clouds from the Miasma Leviathan.
• Food web: salt-loving archaea at the toxic boundary feed plankton such as Brine
Motes, which Halo Brine-Shrimp, Acid Mussels, and other filter feeders sieve out;
Slime-Eels, Brine Tortoises, and other grazers eat the shoreline flora. Brine-Crocodiles
and Corrosive Squid hunt these along the shoreline, and scavengers recycle pickled
carcasses floating on the brine. The Miasma Leviathan filters the brine itself, while the
Sinkhole Wyrm, Pall Kraken, and Pickle Pliosaur hunt the mesopredators.

9. The Void Edge (1,800 m and deeper)
•
•
Layout: the sheer drop-off at the edge of the continental shelf into a bottomless abyss.
No floor and no landmarks, just a cliff face and open dark. Holds the endgame
resources: Void-Crystals and Abyssal Marrow.
Look: endless ink-black in every direction, with distant bioluminescent flashes from
colossal horrors.
Hazards: crushing pressure (needs the Void-Forged Hull), total loss of orientation (the
Solenoid navigation stabilizer helps), energy leeches that drain sub batteries, diver suit
heat, and lights, and the game's largest leviathans.
Food web: there are no true herbivores. Plankton feed on chemical seeps and marine
snow and support filter feeders and light leeches. Fifteen energy leeches drain
electricity, glow, or body heat from flora, fauna, and the crew's subs, and the
mesopredators (including abyssal giants) eat those leeches. Scavengers strip leviathan
falls for Abyssal Marrow, and four apex leviathans led by the Cavernous Maw sweep up
everything in their path.
Flora (20). Strange anchoring lifeforms clinging to the cliff or drifting; all need at least the
Mid-Tier Drill.

smoking volcanic vents. The crew rows there from the Nautilus on the raft.
Why go: it holds land-only resources the sea can't provide: fresh water, timber, resin,
feathers for insulation, venom for medicine, and fruit.
• Cooking fires: players can build fires that cook food more efficiently than on the sub,
but the smoke draws Ash Gulls, Smoke Crows, and the Typhoon-Bird.
• Hazards: typhoons, cliff falls, venomous land animals, mangrove crocodiles that flip
the raft. Sound here is airborne (dB in air), so it doesn't build the ocean's Wake.
• Food web: sunlight powers the jungle, mangrove, and cliff flora, which feed island
grazers such as the Coconut Robber Crab, Thicket Moa, and Frond Iguana. Tide-pool
filter feeders and insects support mesopredators like the Canopy Stalker, Sand-Coil
Viper, Mangrove Monitor, herons, and otters. Smoke-drawn scavengers recycle carrion,
and the Ash Panther, Brackwater Croc, Cinder Dragon, and storm-hunting Typhoon-
Bird sit at the top.

• Unique names: no species name repeats anywhere in the game.
• Closed food webs: every animal's prey and predators are species or flora from its own
biome (plus base foods like plankton, detritus, marine snow, or carrion). Every non-
apex animal is eaten by at least one listed species.
• Sizes and sounds fit the role: plankton are millimeters and silent; apex animals are
huge and loud in the Low band. SOUnd uses the four bands (Low, Mid, High, Ultrasonic);
light Uses lux.
• Yields are separate resources: each named yield is its own item, so recipes can ask for
exactly one part of an animal or plant.
Apex parts are collected, not hunted, where possible: for example, Tangle-Serpent
Scales are shed, and Petrified Silk Cable comes from the Wood-Hag's labyrinths. This
keeps with the pillar that fighting anything bigger than a player is a bad idea.
Wrecks and ruins
Man-made wrecks and ancient ruins create small, intense behavior zones where the
simulation's rules play out in specific ways.
The Sunken Pirate Galleon (Sunlit Shallows)
A decaying 17th-century galleon on a sweeping coral shelf, now a massive artificial reef.
• Ecology: the wooden hull is ideal anchoring for dense Solar-Carpet Algae mats.
Chromis Gliders use the broken gunports as escape points from bait-ball attacks by
Barbed Reef-Snappers.
• Hotspot: its gold and rusted iron cannons draw metal-obsessed Stalker-Hounds up
from deeper water. They nest in the captain's quarters and hoard shiny player drops.
• Danger: tight corridors make speargun shots echo loudly, risking a territorial charge
from a Reef-Crusher Leviathan.
• Salvage: Galleon Wood and Galleon Brass (used in Artisan Bench cosmetics).

The Steam-Powered Naval Vessel (Kelp Labyrinth)
An early 20th-century dreadnought hanging suspended and violently tangled in the iron-
hard kelp.
• Scent masking: the rusted hull leaks ancient heavy-metal lubricants into the current.
This chemical slipstream confuses predators' sense of smell, letting players hide their
own blood trails.
• Structure: Iron-Kelp has grown through the brass propellers and shattered steam
boilers. Players cut through with heated blades to salvage military-grade titanium.
• Danger: the hollow boiler rooms are perfect vertical ambush points for the Tangle-
Serpent Leviathan, which hides its 95 dB constriction roar in the ship's metallic groans.
The breached boilers also cause radiation sickness or heavy-metal poisoning if players
swim too close.
• Salvage: Rusted Iron Scrap, military-grade titanium.
The Abandoned Underwater City (Bioluminescent Caverns)
A sprawling flooded city of unknown origin deep in the cave network, built of smooth,
obsidian-like stone and archways.
• Echo chamber: the geometric stonework amplifies sound. Even a Cave Cleaner
Beetle's 15 dB scuttle is magnified. The crew must stay Dead in the Water crossing the
grand plazas, because the Echo-Ray's 45 kHz clicks bounce endlessly through the
streets.
Light and dark: Lantern Vines and Phosphor Mats overgrow the statues, making
blindingly bright main streets and pitch-black alleys. Flash-Stalker Leviathans corner
prey in the alleys, reflecting their 50,000-IUX strobes off the polished walls to blind
divers from several angles at once.
• Salvage: Smooth Obsidian Stonework, Resonance Stones.
The Crashed Bathysphere Facility (Abyssal Graveyard)
A crushed, highly advanced deep-sea research station lying among the fossil ribcages.
• Mutant blooms: the station's ruptured geothermal reactors give off artificial heat,
causing mutant blooms of Bone-Marrow Sponges on its titanium bulkheads. Abyssal
Scavenger Crabs patrol the wreckage, guarding exposed high-tier mineral caches.
