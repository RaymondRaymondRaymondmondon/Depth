The Bird Exchange
The Market Hall's builder runs the Exchange: a single price board for the whole map,
updated every dawn, where any player can post a buy or sell order for any good and
Traders fill it. The builder takes a fee (1 in 20, set between 1 in IO and 1 in 40) and can
embargo one player a season (their orders don't post). Without a Market Hall, the
Exchange runs from the largest town with no fee and no embargoes.
Market events
The towns' fleet has a bad season (fish prices triple); a Far Sea town is burned by the
navy's enemy (spices vanish); a town's festival wants feathers (the only time feathers are
worth anything); the Sigma's cornering, twice a match now. An Augur predicts them a day
ahead.
Why trade
Pearls are research and Grand Projects; the Far Sea's goods are the only way to afford a
second-tier project before the Reckoning; a trade empire with three routes and a Market
Hall can out-score a war empire without a war. The Sigma's whole year two is this.
Culture: the Chronicle, titles, songs, tools, and taming
Two hours is long enough for a colony to have a story. The Chronicle writes it; titles and
songs are how the colony wears it; tools and taming are what a clever colony learns to do
with its hands and its neighbors.
The Chronicle
Every colony has a book, visible to its player at any time and to everyone at the Reckoning,
written automatically from tagged moments: foundings, first clutches, raids suffered and
given, deaths of Founders and veterans, wonders, wars, successions, marriages, oaths
kept and broken, the Roc's visit, the day the kraken ate the heir. Chapters are seasons; the
prose is generated from templates in the Depth style with the colony's names and the
dynasty's name. At the Reckoning each Chronicle is read aloud (captions) in order of
score. The Chronicle is exported as a text file at the end: the thing the group keeps.
