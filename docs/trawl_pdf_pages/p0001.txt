Overview and pillars
The Trawl is a 1-6 player co-op fishing horror game: a crew works a steam trawler through
three nights per deadline to meet the Owners' quota, while a living food web under the
hull decides what bites, what hunts, and what comes up the rail. It replaces the Trawl
section of the Depth Master Reference (1-4 players, one stub loop) and becomes the full
design Claude Code builds from, the way Fathoms has its own document.
What it takes from each source. From How to Fish, the core loop: catch it, kill it, cook it,
sell it. The rod and lure decide what bites; what comes up fights back; the crew finishes it
with fists, knives, and guns; and a stylish kill (a headshot, an airborne shot, a no-scope, a
spin) multiplies its price through a Killscore. Cooking multiplies it again, boss lures
summon mini-bosses, and money buys better weapons, attachments, and lures. From
Lethal Company: the quota and deadline, a shared boat that the skiff crew leaves behind
to scavenge, a dark interior below decks, a named consignment each deadline, a buy-back
rate that changes by day, monsters with tells and counters, proximity voice, and dead
players who stay USefUl.
Pillars
1. Catch, kill, cook, sell. Every catch is two fights: one on the line and one on the deck.
The Killscore rewards skill and style on the second.
2. Style costs safety. In How to Fish a flashy kill is free. Here, every shot is noise and
every wound is blood that drains through the scuppers into the sea. The kill that pays
most is the one most likely to bring the Great White.
3. The sea is simulated, not scripted. Fish, predators, and threats are agents in one food
web. A monster shows up because something drew it: blood, light, noise, the smoke of
a cooking fire.
4. Greed is the enemy. Brighter lanterns, boss lures, chum, cooking, and deeper water all
pay more and all attract what kills you.
5. Six people, one boat, too many jobs. There are always more stations than hands, so
talking and trusting each other is the game.
