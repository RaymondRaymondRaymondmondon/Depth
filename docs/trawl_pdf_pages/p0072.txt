The Trawl — Arcade Game 2 Design Document (Draft)
The Catch Log. Every species caught is logged with its heaviest weight, and every threat
with a one-line dossier learned from surviving it (the Lethal Company bestiary). A
species' first catch pays a 50% bonus. The Catch Log, the heaviest-fish board, and
trophies mounted in the wheelhouse carry across runs in the arcade profile.
Arcade tokens. 10 per deadline met, 25 for a run past the fourth deadline. Spent on
cosmetics only (boat liveries, lantern colours, suit helmets), per the master reference: the
Trawl never pays into the main game's economy.
Death, injury, and ghosts
Most deaths start with a crewman in the water, so the rescue is a mechanic, not a
cutscene.
Overboard. A player in the water sees a side view of themselves treading water, has 25 s
before cold and exhaustion drown them (12 s in a storm), and is a bleeding, splashing,
noisy thing in the food web. The deck can throw the life ring (a thrown Verlet rope; aim for
the swimmer, then haul), steer the boat alongside (the screw kills swimmers, so the helm
must stop it), or send a second crewman in on a line. Things that took a crewman over the
rail usually aren't done with them.
Injuries (no health bar). Bites, burns, and falls cause injuries that change what a player
can do until the dock: a hooked hand (can't reel), a broken arm (one-handed only, can't
carry big fish), a burn (slower), and a bite (bleeds into the scent grid until bandaged). A
second serious injury in one night is death.
Death. A dead crewman is dead for the night. If their body stays aboard, they revive at the
dock. If the body is lost to the sea, the Owners deduct 8% of the crew's money for the
replacement hand (Lethal Company's rule), and the crewman still returns at the dock.
Ghosts. The dead walk the deck as translucent figures who cannot touch anything except
three things: the ship's bell, the sonar screen (read only), and the ghost's own lantern, a
faint cold light that only other ghosts can see. Ghosts see threats in the dark within 10 m
as pale outlines for a moment after they move, so a dead crewman becomes the best
lookout on the boat. They speak only to other ghosts and through the bell.
Total loss. If every crewman dies, or the boat sinks, the night's catch is lost, the Owners
charge 25% of money for salvaging the Gannet, and the next night starts at the dock. The
run continues unless the quota can no longer be met.
