Warp Dodgeball — Game Design Spec
Balls are picked up automatically by walking over a resting ball on your side. A player can
hold one ball at a time.
Throwing
Throw power is set by how long the throw button is held: a tap lobs, a full charge fires a 26
m/s fastball, and holding too long makes your aim shaky.
Charge
• Charge fills from O to 1 over 1.2 s. A meter around the crosshair shows it.
• Release speed = 8 m/s + 18 m/s x charge2, so short holds stay gentle and the last part of
the charge adds the most speed.
• Weak throws loft upward so they arc: launch angle = camera pitch + 100 at zero charge,
falling to +20 at full charge.
• Overcharge: after 2.0 s held at full, aim spread grows by 20 per second (up to 60). This
stops players camping on a loaded throw.
Charging slows movement to 70%. The release animation takes 0.15 s; the ball leaves
the hand at the end of it.
Curve balls
• Holding curve left/right (or flicking sideways) at the moment of release adds sidespin,
scaled by charge, up to 30 rad/s.
Spin bends the flight path through a Magnus force: F = k x (spin x velocity). Tune k so a
full-curve fastball drifts about 3 m sideways over 20 m.
Spin decays 15% per second and is cut in half by each bounce.
Spin is kept when the ball passes through a portal (rotated with it), so curved portal
shots are possible.
Optional later: vertical flicks for topspin (dips) and backspin (floats).
Practice aid: in practice mode only, show the predicted arc for the first 0.5 s of flight.
Never in matches.
Catching
Catching is a timed button press: hit it inside the window and the thrower is out and a
teammate comes back; miss the timing and you fumble and are out yourself.
