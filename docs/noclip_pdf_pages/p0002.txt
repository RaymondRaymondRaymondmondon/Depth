NOCLIP — Arcade Game 10 Design Document
1. An expansive map. Levels connect to each other and to the Labs; a run is a route
through several levels, not one building.
2. Threshold Labs as outposts with portals, shops, storage, and generators to restart.
3. The Surface: the real world, where loot is sold to a fence for money and to the firm for
the quota, and where the crew buys the next run.
4. Sanity as a second health bar: the Backrooms change what you see and hear the
longer you're in them.
5. Contracts: each run has a job (retrieve a thing, rescue a lost crew, map a level,
photograph an entity) on top of the quota.
Design pillars
1. Liminal first, monsters second. The levels are scary because they're empty, endless,
and almost normal. Entities are rare enough that each one is an event.
2. Greed versus getting out. Every extra minute is more loot and more risk; every Lab is a
choice to cash in or go deeper.
3. Navigation is the skill. Knowing how levels connect, where the exits hide, and how to
get back is what a veteran crew has that a new one doesn't.
4. Funny with friends. Proximity voice, ragdolls, a teammate's voice coming from the
wrong direction, a shopping list that includes a traffic cone.
Session. A run is one "week" of three in-game days, about 45-60 minutes; a full campaign
is runs until the quota beats the crew.
A note on sources. The ten levels and the entities named here are from the Backrooms
community's shared mythology (the Backrooms wiki's level list). The game Uses each
level's premise and mood and invents its own layouts, mechanics, and loot; it doesn't
reproduce wiki text.
The run
A campaign
The crew works for the Liminal Recovery Bureau (the Bureau), a firm that sells
Backrooms objects to collectors and labs. The Bureau sets a quota every week. A week is
three in-game days. Meet the quota by the end of day 3 and the crew is paid and the next
quota is higher; miss it and the Bureau "stops maintaining the portals" for you, which ends
the campaign with the crew left in the Backrooms (a game-over screen in the Bureau's dry
memo style).
