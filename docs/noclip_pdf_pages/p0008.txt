NOCLIP — Arcade Game 10 Design Document
Each restarted Lab is a permanent foothold, which is how a crew pushes deeper week by
week.
Signalling the portal
1. At the control desk, a player starts the signal: a 30 s charge with a rising hum and the
ring spinning up.
2. During the charge the Lab's lights pulse, the hum carries across the level, and entities
that hear it come. Doors can be locked from the desk (if the generator is running); a
breach (an entity through a door, or a Smiler in the dark corners if the lights flicker) is
the crew's fight.
3. The charge can be paused (the hum stops, progress holds) and resumed, which is how
a crew hides from a Hound pack mid-charge.
4. At 100%, the portal opens for 20 s. Everyone in the Portal Hall goes to the Surface; the
crate goes with them. Anyone outside is left behind (and can extract from another Lab
or wait for tomorrow).
5. A Lab's portal needs a cooldown (one in-game hour) after an extraction.
Lab breaches
If an entity gets into the Drop-off while nobody's there, it can tip the crate: a third of the
stored loot is scattered in the Lab and has to be re-dropped, and the most valuable item
may be carried off (it's in the level now, somewhere). Locking the Drop-off's door keeps it
safe as long as the generator runs.
Lab upgrades (bought on the Surface, installed per Lab)
Floodlights (no Smilers inside the Lab), reinforced doors (twice as long to breach), a
second camera bank, a quick-charge coil (20 s charge, but the hum is twice as loud), a fuel
tank (generator runs two days), a cargo link (the Lab's crate can send loot to another
online Lab's crate without extracting, once per hour), and the Siren: a speaker that, when
played, draws every entity in the level to the Lab, which is a terrible idea except for one
contract.
The Lab network
Every online Lab is part of one synced network by default. Restarting a Lab adds it to the
network for the whole crew for the rest of the campaign.
