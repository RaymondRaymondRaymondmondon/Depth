The crew can shop, install Lab upgrades, read the dossier, review the map, sell to the
Fence, and pick tomorrow's insertion Lab. A day ends with a short Bureau memo (a joke
about the day's events, written from the log: "Employee 3 noclipped twice; please stop").
Multiplayer
1-6 players on the shared arcade layer. The crew shares a campaign, a quota, money, and a
map; each player has their own body, meters, and hallucinations.
Proximity voice
• Voice is positional within 25 m, muffled by walls, louder in tunnels and quieter in
carpeted rooms. The radio (shop) carries it across a level.
• Entities hear voices. Talking is noise (a third of a footstep's alarm per second of
speech); shouting is more. Hounds come to voices.
• Skin-Stealers record voices. The game keeps a rolling buffer of each player's recent
proximity speech (on the host, for this purpose only, cleared at the end of the session)
and plays clips from it as the mimic. Voice mimicry is on by default; a lobby toggle
replaces it with synthesized speech for groups who prefer.
• Ghost players (Wanderers) whisper at 3 m.
Roles
No classes; roles emerge from gear and habit. The crew usually settles into: a Guide at a
Lab's Observation Room (cameras, intercom, map), a Lead with the flashlight and chalk,
Haulers with the cart, and a Watcher for Level 9's Neighbors and the portal charge. A solo
player can do it all slowly.
