Scuffle — Arcade Game g Design Document (a Stick Fight game)
Join-in-progress
Anyone can join a match between rounds; a spectator can watch any round with the
replay camera.
The level editor
Stick Fight's long life came from its Workshop. Scuffle's editor is built in, instant, and
shares by code so that no Steam is needed.
Building
A grid of tiles the size of a stick's torso; the stage is 32 x 18 tiles (or 64 x 18 for eight
players), with the camera showing it all.
A palette by world: platforms (stone, wood, ice, glass, rope, chain), movers (elevators
with waypoints, conveyors, rotators, pendulums, pistons on a timer), hazards (every
world's), water, breakables, crate zones, spawn points (2-8), the wall's style, wrap on or
off, and decoration.
• Properties per piece: timers, speeds, ranges, trigger links (a switch opens a door; a
crate landing starts a piston).
• Play now: one key drops you into the stage with bots; the editor stays open; edits apply
live.
• The check: the stage won't save until every spawn can reach every other spawn in the
reachability test (the real movement code), so no stage is unwinnable by accident.
Sharing
• A stage exports as a short text code (the tile data compressed and encoded) that fits
in a chat message; paste it into the lobby and it's in the playlist. No server, no account.
A pack is up to 20 stages in one code.
• The arcade profile keeps a library of stages and packs with names and authors; a stage
played in a match is offered to everyone in the lobby to save.
• Stages carry a world tag so the wall and the crate weights know what to do, and a
difficulty tag the generator's checker estimates (hazard count, platform area, movers).
