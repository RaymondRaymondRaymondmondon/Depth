A Night Off — Arcade Game 6 Design Document
The room
• Crowd bed. A murmur made of layered formant voices whose density follows the
crowd count; it rises at peak and thins at 2 a.m. until individual conversations can be
heard across the room.
• Glass and wood. Clinks, scrapes, a stool dragged, the till drawer, the tab book's pen.
• The jukebox plays six synthesized songs (a shanty, a waltz, a music-hall number, a
torch song, a jig, and the sad one after midnight that the bar sings along to). Requests
cost 10.
• Rain on the yard's string lights and the windows when it starts; the smokers coming in.
• The dog in the alley, and inside once it's YOUrs.
Drunkenness
• Above 40 the mix low-passes slightly and the crowd murmur blurs; above 60 your own
footsteps get heavier and a faint ringing sits under everything; above 80 the room
lurches with the camera and the jukebox is the only clear sound; at a blackout, silence,
then the morning birds.
• Hiccups, a stumble, a glass dropped; the toilet's tap as the sobering sound.
Voices
• Every patron has a formant voice: pitch, rhythm, and a laugh. The regulars' voices are
distinct enough to recognize from across the bar (Big Ruth's laugh, the Reverend's
growl, the Twins in unison).
• Players' crew voices from Red Tide's bark system, reused with new line sets: toasts,
taunts across the pool table, the "I'm fine" at 80 drunk.
Games
Dart thud, the chalk, the cheer for a 180; pool's click and the thunk of a pocket; mini golf's
windmill creak and the fish-tank splash on the ninth; slot reels and the three-kidney
jackpot bell; scratch-off scratching; the fortune teller's cards and a chime per card; poker
chips and the shuffle; bullshit's slap of a called pile.
