Crowd dynamics
• The crowd curve (Dead, Normal, Packed, Random) sets the base numbers per hour;
events add their own people; a fight or a police inspection sends a third of the patrons
home for 20 minutes.
• Patrons arrive in groups (couples, trios, crews) and leave in the groups they came with
or the pairs they made. A patron who went home with a player is gone for the night.
• Rain brings the yard inside; last call empties the games room first; the bartender's last
call shout is the night's last timer.
• The room's noise (a value from the crowd, the jukebox, and fights) sets conversation
difficulty: at peak noise you have to be close to talk, which is why the snug exists.
Multiplayer
One to six players share one bar, one clock, and one crowd. There are no teams by default:
everyone's night is their own, scored on its own, but everything a player does happens in
front of everyone else, which is the point.
What players can do to each other
• Buy each other drinks (and spike them: a Gull in a friend's pint when they're not looking
is +35 to their meter and a story).
• Play each other at every game, bet on each other, and cheat each other.
• Flirt with the same patron (the loser gets a feud and a headline).
• Back each other in fights, or start them: a player can point a Hostile patron at another
player with a line ("he said it").
• Carry a passed-out friend out, or leave them, or draw on their face (a cosmetic that
persists to the morning screen).
