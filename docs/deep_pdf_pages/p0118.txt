• Unique names: no species name repeats anywhere in the game.
• Closed food webs: every animal's prey and predators are species or flora from its own
biome (plus base foods like plankton, detritus, marine snow, or carrion). Every non-
apex animal is eaten by at least one listed species.
• Sizes and sounds fit the role: plankton are millimeters and silent; apex animals are
huge and loud in the Low band. SOUnd uses the four bands (Low, Mid, High, Ultrasonic);
light Uses lux.
• Yields are separate resources: each named yield is its own item, so recipes can ask for
exactly one part of an animal or plant.
Apex parts are collected, not hunted, where possible: for example, Tangle-Serpent
Scales are shed, and Petrified Silk Cable comes from the Wood-Hag's labyrinths. This
keeps with the pillar that fighting anything bigger than a player is a bad idea.
Wrecks and ruins
Man-made wrecks and ancient ruins create small, intense behavior zones where the
simulation's rules play out in specific ways.
The Sunken Pirate Galleon (Sunlit Shallows)
A decaying 17th-century galleon on a sweeping coral shelf, now a massive artificial reef.
• Ecology: the wooden hull is ideal anchoring for dense Solar-Carpet Algae mats.
Chromis Gliders use the broken gunports as escape points from bait-ball attacks by
Barbed Reef-Snappers.
• Hotspot: its gold and rusted iron cannons draw metal-obsessed Stalker-Hounds up
from deeper water. They nest in the captain's quarters and hoard shiny player drops.
• Danger: tight corridors make speargun shots echo loudly, risking a territorial charge
from a Reef-Crusher Leviathan.
• Salvage: Galleon Wood and Galleon Brass (used in Artisan Bench cosmetics).
