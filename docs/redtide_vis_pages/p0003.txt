Three levels of realism.
1. Realistic layer: guns and diving gear. Accurate mechanical parts, true materials (brass,
copper, blued steel, glass, rubber, leather, walnut), wear and corrosion from salt water.
The gun in your hands is the most detailed object on screen.
2. Stylized-realistic layer: creatures, divers' bodies, enemies, bosses. Anatomically
believable, real species read at a glance, slightly exaggerated features for readability
(bigger eyes on prey, bigger teeth on predators), detailed skin, scales, and shells.
3. Stylized layer: environments. Rich and detailed but painterly: clean big shapes, then
dense small detail (coral, weed, barnacles, debris) that sells scale.
The Depth ink identity, refined (decided). Keep the ink line: a thin depth-and-normal
edge line, coloured by the water tint rather than black, fading with distance and fog, so a
screenshot still reads as Depth. The Blender-built detail takes priority over the line:
wherever the line hides or flattens modelled detail (bevels, faces, gun parts, scale
patterns), thin it or drop it locally. Tune line weight per asset class so guns and faces stay
clean. The Bayer stipple is off by default and kept only as a settings toggle, since it fights
the baked texture detail. Both get settings toggles.
Readability rules.
• Every creature's size class, threat, and weak point read at a glance (the design doc's
weak points: gills, eyes, underside, vent, throat get subtle visual emphasis: lighter,
softer, or veined).
• Divers always out-silhouette everything: four fixed shapes, visible at 40 m in clear
water.
• Enemies never look like beasts, and beasts never look like decor. Camouflaged
ambushers are hidden on purpose and get a subtle shimmer tell up close.
• Blood is the game's central resource, so it must always read: dark red ribbons in clear
water, near-black in the deep, never orange or pink.
Blender pipeline
Claude Code builds every asset in Blender through the connected Blender tool, but every
asset must also be reproducible from a committed Python script, so nothing lives only
inside a .blend file that no one can regenerate.
