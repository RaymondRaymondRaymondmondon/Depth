Red Tide — Visual Overhaul Spec
Rendering, effects, and UI
The renderer must make water the main material of the game: light that scatters and
fades with distance and depth, caustics near the surface, and particles everywhere. It
builds on the shared PBR renderer from The Trawl spec, extended in raylib with custom
shaders.
Underwater light.
• Distance and depth fog with per-channel absorption (red disappears first, then green),
so colour shifts toward blue-green with distance and depth, tuned per map and zone.
• Volumetric light shafts from the surface, portholes, and openings, plus caustics
projected onto surfaces near the surface (the design doc's caustics stay; make them
animated and soft).
• PBR shading with cascaded shadows from the sun shaft where it exists, and point and
spot lights with shadows for diver helmet lamps, station lights, lanterns, muzzle
flashes, and bioluminescence.
• Screen-space ambient occlusion and baked AO so creatures and divers sit in the
scene.
• Bioluminescence as emissive materials with bloom, which matters most in the Cave
and the Void.
Particles and water effects.
• Marine snow everywhere (the design doc's rule), denser in the deep.
• Bubbles from helmets, guns, slipstreams, vents, and struggling creatures, rising and
wobbling.
• Silt clouds when divers or beasts touch the bottom; shells and coral chips from hits.
• Blood as ribbon particles following the scent grid's flow (from the design doc), dark red
and thick in clear water, and black ichor for the Lost Ones, iridescent oil for Sentinels.
• Air pockets with a mirrored, rippling surface from below, and a dry, dripping world
above.
Post-processing. HDR, filmic tonemapping, bloom on bright sources, a gentle lens
distortion and vignette at the helmet edges, chromatic fringing at the very edge of the
port, and the design doc's red tint at the mask edge as local scent rises. The refined ink
line from section 2 is applied here, after fog, so it fades with the water.
