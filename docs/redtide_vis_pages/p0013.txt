Red Tide — Visual Overhaul Spec
• Token skins (barnacle, verdigris, bone inlay, pearl, red tide) are material sets on every
gun, with real geometry where the name implies it: barnacles sit on the surface, bone
inlay is set into the stock.
Viewmodels, animation, and underwater effects
• Both hands always grip the gun correctly. A separate viewmodel FOV (55-650). Aiming
down sights aligns real sights or scopes with the eye.
• Every gun has draw, holster, idle sway (slower and floatier than on land), aim, fire, dry
fire, partial and empty reloads, a Speed Brine reload 30% faster, and an inspect that
shows the Forge finish, exactly as the design doc's Animations section lists.
• Reloads are mechanically true: gas pistols pop the cylinder and thumb in darts one at a
time; needlers swap a glass cartridge with a hiss of bubbles; powder guns eject a
sealed brass clip that sinks and glints; spearguns cock with both arms straining;
harpoon rifles winch a bolt from the quiver; the scatter gun loads shell by shell.
• Firing underwater: gas guns release a burst of bubbles from vents; powder guns give a
brass flash and a shock ring in the water; spears and bolts leave a cavitation trail and a
line snapping taut. Recoil is damped and drifts as in water. Spent cases and clips sink
with physics.
• Hits on beasts release blood ribbons and scales; misses kick up silt or chip coral.
• The third-person model of every gun (held by teammates and enemies) shows the
same finish, attachments, and Forge state at lower detail.
Fish, beasts, and flora
All -200 beasts and -60 flora must read as their real species at a glance, look alive in
motion, and show the ecosystem's state (hungry, fed, wounded, fleeing, dead) on their
bodies. Use the creature kit from section 3 for the common work and give hero species
individual attention.
Anatomy standard
• Correct body plan and proportions per species: fin count and placement, gill slits or
plates, the lateral line, eyes set where the species has them, mouths that open to the
right shape, teeth, barbels, spines, and claws.
Eyes are separate meshes with a wet specular highlight and, for predators, a reflective
tapetum that glints in diver lamps.
