Red Tide — Visual Overhaul Spec
HUD. Keep the design doc's brass-helmet HUD and make it a real model: the inside of the
helmet rendered with brass, rivets, and glass, gauges as real dials with needles (the scent
meter, the predator pulse, air, scrip as a mechanical counter), condensation and droplets
on the glass, and cracks when the diver is badly hurt. Menus, the dossier, and the how-to-
play page get matching period design: aged paper, brass, typewriter and engraved type.
Accessibility and readability. A brightness and fog-density calibration screen; a
colourblind setting for blood, ichor, and scent colours; and a motion-comfort setting for
viewmodel sway and lens effects.
Process and acceptance
Work in ten phases, building one complete map (the Sunken Ship) to full quality before
scaling to the other four. Each phase ends with before/after renders that Ray approves
before the next starts.
Screenshot harness. Extend the design doc's -- redtide-shots (every creature,
weapon, and map zone to PNG) to render at with the new renderer, from fixed
cameras. Also render turntables from Blender for every new asset before it enters the
game. Claude Code looks at both, compares them with this spec, and fixes problems
before showing Ray. The fixed shots are:
1. Every map zone from its entrance, showing its landmark.
2. Each diver: front, side, back, a face close-up through the helmet port, and each skin.
3. Each gun: side and three-quarter views on a neutral background, the viewmodel at
rest, aiming down sights, and mid-reload, plus the Forged version.
4. Each faction: a full squad lined up, then the same squad in combat.
5. Each boss: a full-body hero shot and one shot per phase.
6. Each hero species, plus a sample of kit species, in its habitat.
7. A busy combat moment per map: four divers, a squad, beasts, blood in the water.
