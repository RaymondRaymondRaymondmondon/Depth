Red Tide — Visual Overhaul Spec
The maps
Each map becomes a dense, handcrafted-looking place built from a Blender modular kit
and placed by the existing layout data. The zones, routes, doors, and costs in the design
doc stay exactly where they are. Every zone gets a landmark visible from its entrance, so
players learn the map by sight.
Rules for every map
Big shapes, then small detail. Strong readable architecture or rock forms first, then
dense layered set dressing: weed, barnacles, sediment, debris, flora, and drifting
particles.
• Interactables stand out. Racks (weapons chalked on walls), Davy's Locker (with its
lantern buoy), tonic machines, the Forge, workbenches, doors with their scrip prices,
traps, and power switches each get a distinct, well-lit, detailed model that never gets
lost in the decor.
• Habitats look like habitats. Where a species lives (the crocodile's lair, the moray pipes,
the eel holes, the cleaning station) the set dressing shows it: bones, nests, cleared
sand, shed shells.
• The map responds. Blood drifts with the current; shot coral chips; corpses sink and get
picked over; doors bought open stay open with debris pushed aside.
Per-map treatment
The Sunken Ship. The Marguerite as a real 60 m steam yacht on her side at 30 m: hull
plates with rivets, a funnel, masts and rigging trailing kelp, portholes throwing light shafts.
Inside, the tilt matters: furniture piled against the low walls, the grand salon's chandelier
hanging sideways, the air pocket under the tilted ceiling with a silver surface. Rotting
mahogany panelling, peeling wallpaper, tarnished brass, a velvet-and-gilt salon, a tiled
galley, cabins with personal belongings, a riveted engine room with the generator and the
Goliath's nest. Outside: the sand shelf, the keel, and the sharks' approach lane fading into
blue. The foredeck crane and container trap are visible from the salon.
