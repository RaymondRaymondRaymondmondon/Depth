The map's monsters
• The kraken is a persistent agent: it sleeps unless blood or noise wakes it, grabs low
birds with arms, and surfaces fully in storms or when offered nothing for three days. It
can be fought (a Bomber campaign and a lot of blood) for the cove's hoard; killing it is a
match-defining event and the cove's yield halves without it (nothing keeps the sharks
out).
• The great ape on skull island throws rocks at low flyers and sleeps when fed. It can't be
killed; it can be lured to another player's nests with a trail of fish.
• The giant grouper under the sea stack eats anything that falls; it is the stack's moat.
Birds in the web
Wild birds (gulls, frigatebirds, terns, boobies) live on every map, fish the same grounds,
steal from caches, and are the pool mates come from. A colony that kills the wild birds
near it gets fewer mates. Priests convert wild birds into colony birds (one per game day
per priest), which is the Ibis founder's growth engine.
Scouting, the map, and reports
YOU know what your birds have seen. The map (the M key, or the chart button) shows the
world as your colony has observed it: islands YOU've landed on in full, islands you've flown
over as silhouettes, grounds your fishers have worked with their yields and predator
pressure, and everything else as fog. Enemy colonies appear as what your SCOUtS last
reported, with a timestamp.
The map screen
• A hand-drawn chart in the ink style, revealed in patches by every bird's sight. Fog is
cloud; old information fades to sepia (a nest count from two days ago is drawn faded,
with "seen 2 days ago").
