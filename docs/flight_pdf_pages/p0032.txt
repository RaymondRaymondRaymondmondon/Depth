The Flight — Arcade Game 7 Design Document
Simulation and networking
• The host runs the ecosystem (Red Tide's engine), the bird Al (schedules, hunger, roles,
flocks), and the economy. About 600 birds and 2,000 fish at full rate near players;
population counters elsewhere.
• Host-authoritative through the shared arcade layer: the Founder's flight is predicted
locally; orders are commands; everything else is state at 20 Hz with interest
management by distance and fog. Six players is the design limit for the bird count.
• Disconnects: the colony runs on its last orders for 2 minutes, then an Al takes it (a
catJtiOUS one: it fishes and defends).
Sound
• The sea and the wind are the bed: surf by island type, wind rising with altitude, rigging
on the town's boats, the cove's slow breathing when the kraken sleeps.
• Birds have voices per species (a cry, a call, a chick's peep) and the colony has a chorus
that swells with its size: a colony of 100 is loud, which is how a SCOUt hears one before
seeing it.
• Fishing: the dive's whistle, the strike's splash, the struggle, the fish slapping the nest.
Feed shortages are heard as hungry peeps before the panel flashes.
• War: wingbeats in formation, the Striker's dive shriek, the net's whip, the bomb's thud
and the hedge burning, the Screamer's scream, a flock routing (a scatter of cries), the
kraken's roar when it surfaces.
• Reports arrive as a call and a chalk scribble; the map is a paper rustle.
• Music: a windy brass-and-strings theme that layers with colony size and a war motif
that enters when flocks engage; a dawn chorus and a dusk hush each game day; the
kraken has a theme nobody wants to hear.
• Founders have a signature call each (the Lyrebird mimics the last sound it heard; the
Screamer-bird's is the Screamer's).
