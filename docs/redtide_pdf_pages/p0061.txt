Presentation and technology
Red Tide is first-person 3D in raylib, extending the code that already draws the parkour
side's 3D Abyss ( abyss . cpp ), with every mesh, texture, and animation generated in code,
per Depth's no-assets rule. The look is inked low-poly: flat-shaded meshes with a screen-
space ink outline and hatching, so a screenshot still reads as Depth.
Art
Style. Low-poly meshes with flat shading, a post-process ink outline (depth and normal
edge detection, line weight by distance), Bayer stipple in shadow, and the same paper-
tone palette rules as the salon. Underwater fog by depth, volumetric light shafts,
caustics projected on surfaces near the surface, and particle marine snow everywhere.
• Creatures are procedural meshes. A CreatureBui1der makes a body from a lathed
profile curve, adds fins, limbs, shells, tentacles, spines, and eyes from parameter sets,
and generates a 3-band material (base, belly, accent) from the species record. Forty
species per map is forty parameter sets plus a dozen body plans (fusiform fish, flat
fish, eel, ray, shark, turtle, crocodilian, crab, shrimp, cephalopod, jelly, worm, amphibian,
seal, whale).
• Animation. Fish swim by a traveling sine along the spine; rays flap; eels undulate; crabs
walk on inverse-kinematics legs; cephalopods run Chains on every arm; reptiles and
mammals use a four-limb gait. Attack, flee, feed, and death clips per body plan; blood
as ribbon particles that follow the scent grid's flow so what the player sees is what the
web smells.
• Weapons and hands. First-person hands and guns are procedural meshes with the
reload clips from the Weapons section; the sea-glass Forge finish is a material swap.
